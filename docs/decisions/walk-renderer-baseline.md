# Walk renderer baseline and software fallback

2 November 2026. The route study has 39 outlined spaces and 17 source-feature-referenced openings. The private plans and test project remain ignored by Git.

**Decision:** Keep the full raster scene on the Apple M1. On a WebGL context that reports SwiftShader, Walk uses a 0.5 pixel ratio, omits decorative landscape models and photographic texture maps, draws installed asset footprints as simple boxes, and disables the shadow pass. The same spatial model supplies all walls, gaps, floors and stair treads. A Blender export loads the full asset models on demand, so the software preview does not silently replace export geometry. No new library was added.

**Alternatives and experiment:** At an 880 × 720 CSS viewport, the in-app browser reported `ANGLE Metal Renderer: Apple M1`. A five-second held-W sample at 1760 × 1440 render pixels measured **18.6 ms** p95 between frames, **457** draw calls and **1,041,062** triangles. Lowering pixel ratio to 1.5 alone measured **19.3 ms** p95. Disabling shadows alone measured **18.7 ms** p95 despite cutting draw calls to 152. Both one-change experiments were reverted on the M1 because they did not improve the observed frame interval.

A separate temporary headless Chrome profile used `--use-angle=swiftshader`; the canvas reported `SwiftShader Device`, confirming software WebGL. With the full scene at 1760 × 1440, the five-second hold produced only **4 movement ticks** and **2,454.5 ms** p95 between sampled frames. Lowering pixels to 880 × 720 and disabling shadows yielded 9 ticks and 1,325 ms p95. Replacing asset models and photographic maps with plain shapes reduced this to 16 ticks and 824.1 ms p95. Removing the decorative plant models reduced triangles from about 115,000 to about 2,000. At a 440 × 360 render size, the repeat test recorded **108 movement ticks** in five seconds, **50.7 ms** frame-interval p95, about **128** draw calls and **1,942** triangles. Project import took **5,026 ms** in that run. The fixed held-key route reached Bedroom 2 at 3.20 m with no collision stop. A 0.75 pixel ratio measured 107.6 ms p95 and did not finish that timed route; 0.6 measured 73 ms p95 and stopped short of the bedroom. The 0.5 fallback remains in place.

The software Walk screenshot shows the avatar, bedroom furniture and openings clearly enough to follow the route, but it is visibly coarse. Export from that same software session produced a 3.7 MB GLB. Its JSON had **545 nodes, 402 mesh definitions and 108 stair-tagged nodes**, matching the full-detail browser export; Blender 5.2.1 imported **445 mesh objects and 313,520 vertices** from both files. The asset placeholders therefore affect the software preview, not its exported geometry.

The normal M1 path was retested after the fallback: 1760 × 1440 render pixels, **18.5 ms** frame p95, 460 calls and 1,041,098 triangles; the held-key stair route still reached 3.20 m without a stop. These are short local runs on one physical Apple M1 and one temporary SwiftShader process, not a second-device confirmation. The dev-only canvas probe reports renderer, frame-interval p95, CPU render-submission p95, calls and triangles while moving. Frame intervals include browser scheduling; CPU submission is not GPU time. The software fallback is selected through `WEBGL_debug_renderer_info`, so a browser that hides the renderer identity may not choose it. A browser without WebGL cannot run this Three.js viewer. Final frame and load targets still need confirmation on a second physical device.

**Provisional visit targets after the baseline:** at an 880 × 720 CSS viewport, reach Bedroom 2 by walking from the third-floor entrance without relocation or collision stop; measure moving-frame p95 at or below 33 ms on an integrated GPU and 80 ms in the software-WebGL fallback, with project import at or below 10 s. These thresholds are above the observed M1 and SwiftShader samples but still require a second physical-device run and a longer route before acceptance. The local SwiftShader process reported about 31 MB JavaScript heap after its v2 route; this is not total browser or GPU memory, so no memory pass target is claimed from it.

A follow-up camera experiment tried a longer minimum boom distance and sampled whether the camera point intersected any modeled wall after each held-key route segment. It reached Bedroom 2, but a cutaway fourth-floor lintel intersected the eased camera point at the stair turn, and a wall dominated one bedroom frame. The distance change was reverted. Reducing only the opacity of the existing ray-blocking wall fade from 0.12 to 0.05 made the bedroom and avatar more legible in the same route, which again reached 3.20 m. This does not close the camera gate: a cutaway wall can still contain the camera position, and the exposed stair assembly looks unsupported against the open landscape. Those need geometry and camera review before showcase approval.

An upper-wardrobe branch then reached Toilet 02 at 3.20 m, but its CC0 shelving filled the camera frame. The Walk camera now checks cached asset bounds along head and torso sightlines and temporarily hides the matching asset set within that room while it blocks the avatar. On the same route the toilet doorway and avatar were visible; the four wardrobe bookcases were hidden in Walk and restored when the camera moved away. A GLB exported while they were hidden still contained all four bookcase nodes and 108 stair-tagged nodes. A fresh SwiftShader run with this camera change reached Bedroom 2 at 3.20 m, 54.3 ms moving-frame p95, 129 calls and 1,954 triangles at 440 × 360 pixels. This is a local cutaway test, not a complete camera or second-device review.

The default boom rise was reduced from 0.55 to 0.3 times follow distance. Local Pooja and Bedroom 2 frames show the avatar and destination closer to eye level, rather than looking over the wall tops. A fresh SwiftShader visit on the strict v2 project reached Bedroom 2 at 3.20 m, 63.4 ms moving-frame p95 at 440 × 360 pixels, 235 calls and 3,226 triangles. The new angle has not been reviewed in every room; the stair assembly still looks unsupported against the open landscape. The dev-only collision probe now preserves an axis block when movement slides on the other axis.

The camera's target distance was previously checked against visible walls, but easing toward it could still put the intermediate camera point inside a wall. The eased point is now moved to the nearest clear position along the same boom whenever that happens. A fresh saved-v2 M1 browser route from the east entrance through the stair to Bedroom 2 ended at x≈6.15 m, 3.20 m elevation, z≈3.57 m without a collision stop. The development camera-inside-wall probe was false after each of its 14 route segments, and the final bedroom frame showed the avatar, bed and toilet doorway. Its last moving-frame probe reported **26.4 ms p95** and 689 calls on `ANGLE Metal Renderer: Apple M1`, within the provisional 33 ms integrated-GPU target. This checks sampled segment endpoints and a short frame window; it does not establish that every intermediate frame or branch is clear. SwiftShader has not been rerun after this camera correction.

Continuing from Bedroom 2 into its wardrobe and Toilet 02 showed the new camera probe still false. The avatar needed a 0.36 m east alignment after the wardrobe's north wall stopped forward motion, then entered Toilet 02 and stopped against the basin furniture at x≈8.87 m, z≈0.89 m. The default forward view in this small toilet mostly shows the wall behind the avatar; a tighter camera composition or reviewer-approved orbit is still needed for full room context.

Another upper-level browser visit took a furniture-aware detour from the fourth-floor landing through the family room, transition and wardrobe into Toilet 02 without a floor relocation. At x≈9.05 m, z≈0.86 m, the basin stopped forward travel and the camera-inside-wall probe stayed false. The straight-ahead frame showed mostly the doorway and wall. Dragging the view about 0.6 radians to the left showed the avatar, toilet, basin and room floor together while retaining a false camera-inside-wall probe. The room is therefore viewable with the existing orbit control, but the default composition is still weak and remains for visual review.

The upper master-bedroom branch exposed another obstruction: the default camera looked across the bed and hid the avatar's legs. Walk now fades only box furniture pieces intersecting a lower-body-to-camera sightline, restoring their materials after the view changes. A fresh entrance-to-stair-to-master-bedroom browser run ended at x≈5.15 m, elevation 3.20 m, z≈8.17 m with no collision stop or camera-in-wall probe. The mattress and one bed accent became translucent in that frame, revealing the avatar while the bed frame and pillows remained visible. Its last moving-frame probe was **27.6 ms p95** on the M1, below the provisional 33 ms target. A GLB exported while those two pieces were faded retained all six master-bedroom bed nodes; the two affected primitives were opaque with alpha 1 in the exported file. This is a focused camera and export check, not approval across every room.

After the furniture cutaway and estimated balcony guards, a fresh headless Chrome run using ANGLE SwiftShader reopened saved v2 in **4,848 ms** and walked the east entrance through the physical stair into Bedroom 2, ending at x≈5.79 m, elevation 3.20 m, z≈3.77 m with no final collision stop. The 880 × 720 CSS viewport rendered at 440 × 360 pixels. A five-second movement sample reported **55 ms p95** between frames, 235 calls and 3,226 triangles. This meets the provisional software target on the same Mac, but the resulting view is visibly coarse and does not substitute for a second physical device or an independent visual verdict.
